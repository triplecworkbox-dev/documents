Interview purpose

Assess whether the candidate can own the technical product direction for an agentic AI healthcare product—not merely coordinate delivery. The successful candidate should translate business and clinical needs into a safe, reliable and commercially viable product; understand AI agents, workflows, integrations, data and system architecture; engage credibly with engineering; and manage risk.

Interviewer guidance: Ask for concrete examples, the candidate’s personal contribution, alternatives considered and measurable outcomes. Distinguish what they personally owned from what engineers, architects, clinicians or data specialists delivered.

4. Practical scenario: Agentic AI healthcare MVP

Read to the candidate: We want to launch an agentic AI healthcare product. A patient interacts with the product, provides information, receives assistance, and may trigger actions such as booking an appointment, routing a query, summarising information for a clinician or updating a connected system. The product may integrate with clinical systems and must operate safely, securely and reliably.

Ask the candidate to work through

  1. Define the primary user problem, intended use and target users. What must be clarified before solution design?
  2. Map the end-to-end user journey and identify which steps are agent-led, deterministic or human-led.
  3. Propose a high-level architecture: agent orchestration, models, tools, APIs, knowledge retrieval, data stores and monitoring.
  4. Identify key integrations and explain authentication, permissions, errors and retries.
  5. Write five initial user stories. For one AI capability, define measurable acceptance criteria and safety tests.
  6. Define the evaluation plan: test data, success measures, failure categories, red-team cases and release thresholds.
  7. Identify clinical, privacy, security, operational and commercial risks, with mitigations.
  8. Propose a phased MVP roadmap, including a controlled pilot and criteria for expanding autonomy.

Complication to introduce

The CEO wants the agent to autonomously assess symptoms, advise patients and book appointments. The clinical lead is concerned that the system may miss urgent cases or provide unsafe advice. The launch date is three months away. What would you do?

Listen for intended-use clarification; clinical accountability; risk-tiering; appropriate scope; deterministic emergency/escalation pathways; human review for higher-risk cases; robust evaluation; safe fallback; privacy/security controls; staged pilot; monitoring and go/no-go criteria. A disclaimer or prompt instruction alone is not a sufficient safety control.

6. Hiring calibration & interviewer notes

Delivery-focused PO: tracks sprint progress and ticket completion, passes technical questions to developers, treats AI output as a feature and assumes launch means feature-complete.

Technically capable AI healthcare PO: owns product outcomes and technical trade-offs, interrogates agent architecture and failure modes, defines evaluation and guardrails, and balances value, clinical risk, reliability, privacy and operational readiness.
